# OFDM-PAPR Reduction

OFDM is a special multi - carrier modulation and multiplexing technique. That is, it converts high - speed data streams through serial - to - parallel conversion and distributes them to several frequency sub - channels with relatively low rates for transmission. This maximally improves the spectrum utilization rate. Meanwhile, it can also effectively combat multipath fading and flexibly allocate spectrum resources.  
However, since the OFDM signal in the time domain is the superposition of multiple orthogonal sub - carriers, when the signals are added in - phase or out - of - phase, the instantaneous peak power will reach extremely high or low values. That is, the peak power of the signal is much higher than its average power, which is simply called the Peak - to - Average Power Ratio (PAPR). This leads to large variations in the amplitude envelope of the signal. As a result, it requires a larger linear range for the signal amplifier, reduces the energy utilization efficiency of the power amplifier, and makes the signal more prone to distortion.  
Therefore, whether in the current 5G technology or future wireless communication technologies, how to effectively manage and reduce PAPR has become one of the important research directions for improving the performance of OFDM systems.
